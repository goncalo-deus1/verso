# Living in Almada: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Almada is the fifth most populous municipality in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area, with 177,000 residents and a median T2 rent between €850 and €950. It is 5 minutes from Lisboa by ferry and 10 minutes via the Ponte 25 de Abril, which makes the Cacilhas-Trafaria-Cova da Piedade strip one of the best value-for-money-to-travel-time options in the AML. A good choice for people working in central or western Lisboa who enjoy the beach. Less suitable for those who need to reach Saldanha or the airport daily without using the metro.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Almada worth living in?

Almada has three distinct realities. The northern riverside strip (Cacilhas, Cova da Piedade, Pragal) is urban and well served by transport to Lisboa. The central area (Almada-Cidade, Laranjeiro) is residential and family-oriented. The Costa da Caparica area is more touristic and has the best beach access on the south bank.

The combination of proximity to Lisboa, lower costs than the north bank and beach access makes Almada particularly attractive to young couples and creative professionals.

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Almada in 2026?

The median rent per square metre in Almada is around €10 to €11, according to INE data for the Setúbal Peninsula. For an 80 m² T2, the rent sits between €850 and €950 per month in the more central areas closer to Lisboa.

The most expensive areas are Cacilhas, Pragal (with a train station and Metro Sul do Tejo) and the riverside front. The more affordable ones are Charneca da Caparica, Sobreda and inland parts of Laranjeiro.

Costa da Caparica has its own market, with a strong seasonal component. Annual rent for a T2 can range between €800 and €1,200, depending on proximity to the beach.

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Base scenario: accumulated growth between 12% and 18%, taking the median T2 rent to between €950 and €1,100 per month by 2029. Almada is benefiting from the spillover effect from Lisboa and the greater attractiveness of the south bank. The projects of the Arco Ribeirinho Sul (now Parque Cidades do Tejo) could further drive demand. Risk of an even larger rise if the Ocean Campus moves forward on the expected timeline.

## Population and density

Almada had 177,400 residents in the 2021 Census, 1.9% more than in 2011. The area is 70.21 km², which gives a density of 2,527 inhabitants per km².

The population is growing mainly in Costa da Caparica and Sobreda, while Cacilhas and Cova da Piedade maintain stable density.

## Who lives in Almada? Resident profile

Almada attracts an interesting mix. The northern strip (Cacilhas, Cova da Piedade) is sought after by young professionals and the creative class who want proximity to Lisboa for less money. Costa da Caparica and Charneca da Caparica attract families and surfers. The central areas have an established ageing population, but are rejuvenating with the arrival of young families.

The presence of Brazilian and African communities is significant, especially in Almada-Cidade and Laranjeiro.

## Transport and connection to the centre of Lisboa

Almada has three main transport options to Lisboa:

**Third Tagus Crossing** — `Announced`
New Lisboa-Chelas to Barreiro link with road and rail components, announced in 2024. The direct impact on Almada is smaller than for Barreiro, but it will relieve pressure on the Ponte 25 de Abril.

Ferry (Cacilhas-Cais do Sodré): the fastest link to the centre of Lisboa. About 8 to 10 minutes of crossing, with a 10-minute frequency at peak times.

Metro Sul do Tejo + train: the Metro Sul do Tejo connects several areas of Almada and Seixal. Pragal station has a Fertagus train connection to Lisboa (Roma-Areeiro, Sete Rios, Entrecampos).

Car via the Ponte 25 de Abril: the most flexible option, but the most subject to congestion at peak times. Off-peak, it takes 10 to 15 minutes to reach western Lisboa.

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

**Ocean Campus (Margueira)** — `Announced`
Transformation of the former Lisnave shipyards in Cacilhas into a hub for housing, marine science and public space. Oversight was transferred to Parque Cidades do Tejo, S.A. by the Decree-Law of September 2025.

The Arco Ribeirinho Sul, a set of urban regeneration projects between Almada, Barreiro and Seixal, gained new momentum with the creation of Parque Cidades do Tejo.

Almada has 280 families on the waiting list for municipal housing. The City Council acknowledges significant housing pressure.

The refurbishment of the riverside front between Cacilhas and Cova da Piedade continues, with investment in public space.

## Schools, healthcare and retail

In education, Almada has the main campus of Universidade Nova de Lisboa (Faculty of Sciences and Technology) at Monte de Caparica, attracting thousands of students. Public and private provision is good, with references such as Colégio São João de Brito (in Lisboa, but close by) and Colégio Atlântico.

In healthcare, Hospital Garcia de Orta is the public reference and serves the entire south bank. Private provision includes Hospital Particular de Almada and CUF Almada.

In retail, Almada Forum is the largest shopping centre and one of the main ones in the AML. Centro Comercial Cacilhas, municipal markets and the high streets of Cacilhas and Almada-Cidade round out the offer.

## FAQ

### Is Almada good for families?
Yes. Sobreda, Charneca da Caparica and Almada-Cidade have good schools and parks. Charneca has more space and gardens. T3 rent between €1,000 and €1,300.

### Is it worth living in Almada if I work in central Lisboa?
It makes a lot of sense if you use the ferry or the train. From Cacilhas to Cais do Sodré is 8 to 10 minutes by ferry. From Almada to Roma-Areeiro is 25 minutes by train.

### Almada or Seixal: which is better?
Almada has a direct ferry to Lisboa, Almada Forum and Costa da Caparica. Seixal is cheaper, more residential and has its own identity. Almada for those who prioritise proximity to Lisboa, Seixal for those who prioritise price.

### What is Ocean Campus?
It is the urban regeneration project of the former Lisnave shipyards in Cacilhas, which will transform 50 hectares into a zone of housing, science and public space. It has been under Parque Cidades do Tejo's oversight since September 2025.

### Is Costa da Caparica good to live in all year round?
For those who enjoy the beach and are willing to accept some isolation in winter, yes. Transport to Lisboa is reasonable (bus and Metro Sul do Tejo). In summer there is more activity but also more traffic and tourist occupation.
