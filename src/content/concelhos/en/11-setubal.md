# Living in Setúbal: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Setúbal is the southernmost district capital in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area, with 124,000 residents and a median T2 rent between €750 and €900. It has the Fertagus train to Lisboa in around 50 minutes and is close to the Serra da Arrábida and the Sado beaches. A good choice for those working remotely or in a hybrid model with few trips to Lisboa, and for those who prioritise natural quality of life. Less suitable for those who need to reach central Lisboa daily during rush hour without spending an hour going from home to the office.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Setúbal worth living in?

Setúbal has a life of its own. It does not function as a metropolitan suburb of Lisboa, but as a mid-sized city with a strong identity. It has the Sado Estuary, the Serra da Arrábida, Figueirinha beach, rich gastronomy (fried cuttlefish, fresh fish) and a significantly lower cost of living than most of the Lisbon Metropolitan Area.

Housing pressure has increased substantially in recent years, with Setúbal recording one of the largest rent increases in the country (+7.6% in 2025 according to Idealista).

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Setúbal in 2026?

The median rent per square metre in Setúbal rose 7.6% in 2025 according to the Idealista index. For an 80 m² T2, the rent ranges between €750 and €900 per month.

The most expensive areas are the rehabilitated historic centre, Avenida Luísa Todi and zones near the sea (close to Doca dos Pescadores). The most affordable are São Sebastião, Azeitão (on the border with Sesimbra) and inland areas.

Per square metre for sale, Setúbal had a median of €2,320, according to INE, significantly below Lisboa (€4,691) or Cascais (€4,477).

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Base scenario: accumulated growth between 18% and 25%, taking the median T2 rent to between €900 and €1,100 per month by 2029. Setúbal is being strongly affected by the spillover effect from Lisboa and by the growing attractiveness of the south bank. Idealista identifies it as one of the mid-sized cities with the largest rent increases in 2025. Risk of accelerated growth if the new airport in Alcochete and the Third Tagus Crossing energise the south bank.

## Population and density

Setúbal had 123,684 residents in the 2021 Census, 2.1% more than in 2011. The area is 230.33 km², with a density of 537 inhabitants per km².

The population is concentrated in the city of Setúbal and in Azeitão. The rural parishes (Sado, Gâmbia-Pontes-Alto da Guerra) are sparsely populated.

## Who lives in Setúbal? Resident profile

Setúbal has a middle-class and established working-class profile. There is an industrial base (Autoeuropa in Palmela, but with strong links to Setúbal; the historic Lisnave; the Mitrena steelworks) that underpins the social structure.

In recent years, Setúbal has been attracting Lisboa's creative class, expatriates and remote workers seeking price, quality of life and proximity to the beach. The international presence is still smaller than in Lisboa or Cascais, but it has been growing.

## Transport and connection to central Lisboa

Setúbal has three main options for Lisboa:

**Third Tagus Crossing** — `Post-2030`
The rail component of the new crossing should significantly improve the Setúbal-Lisboa journey time once completed.

The Fertagus train connects Setúbal to Lisboa (Roma-Areeiro, Sete Rios, Entrecampos). From Setúbal to Roma-Areeiro it takes around 55 minutes. Frequency is every 30 minutes during rush hour.

By car along the A2 and the Vasco da Gama Bridge or the 25 de Abril Bridge, the A2 is the fastest connection (around 45 minutes to Lisboa outside rush hour). During rush hour, it can take 1h15.

Several TST express coaches connect Setúbal to Lisboa, mainly to Sul/Sueste and Pragal (with a connection to the metro and Fertagus).

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

Setúbal has 1,963 families on the waiting list, the third largest number in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area after Lisboa and Sintra. There are Recovery and Resilience Plan projects in execution.

The rehabilitation of Setúbal's riverfront continues, including Doca dos Pescadores and the tourist pier.

New private developments are being marketed, mainly in the eastern part of the city and in Azeitão.

Investment continues in nature tourism amenities (dolphin watching, hiking trails in the Arrábida).

## Schools, healthcare and retail

In education, Setúbal has the Polytechnic Institute of Setúbal with several schools (Technology, Education, Health, Business), attracting thousands of students. Public and private provision is good.

In healthcare, São Bernardo Hospital is the public reference. Private provision includes Hospital da Luz Setúbal and private clinics.

In retail, Setúbal Retail Park, Alegro Setúbal and the historic centre cover the offering. Mercado do Livramento is considered one of the most beautiful municipal markets in the world.

## FAQ

### Is Setúbal good for families?
Yes. Azeitão, São Sebastião and residential areas west of the centre have good schools, parks and proximity to the beach. T3 rent between €900 and €1,100.

### Is Setúbal worth it if I work in Lisboa?
It makes more sense for those who work remotely or in a hybrid arrangement. For those who have to go to central Lisboa every day, total commuting time is around 1h45 to 2h per day.

### Setúbal or Almada: which is better?
Almada is closer to Lisboa and has a ferry. Setúbal has more identity of its own, more space, better access to the Arrábida and the Sado, and an even lower cost of living. Almada for proximity to Lisboa, Setúbal for natural quality of life.

### Which are the cheapest areas in Setúbal?
São Sebastião and inland areas away from the centre have T2 rents between €600 and €750.

### Is there night-time public transport?
Night-time provision is limited. The last Fertagus trains leave around 11pm. At night, you mostly depend on a private car or taxi.
