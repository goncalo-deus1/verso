# Living in Mafra: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Mafra is the municipality in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area that has grown the most in population over the last decade (+12.8%), with 87,000 residents and a median T2 rent between €750 and €900. It has highly sought-after coastal areas (Ericeira, Foz do Lizandro) for quality of life and inland areas (Mafra-Vila, Malveira, Venda do Pinheiro) with a rural-urban profile. A good choice for young families who want a house with land and a less urban environment. Less suitable for those who need to reach central Lisboa daily by public transport.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Mafra worth living in?

Mafra has grown more than 12% in ten years, proof that many people are willing to give up proximity to Lisboa in exchange for more space, clean air and beach access (Ericeira). It is a municipality with two realities: the coastal strip (Ericeira) is expensive, touristy and has a strong international presence. The interior (Mafra-Vila, Malveira, Venda do Pinheiro) is more residential and has more housing on offer.

The biggest limitation is the lack of a train. The connection to Lisboa is 100% by road.

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Mafra in 2026?

The median T2 rent in Mafra is between €750 and €900 per month in the most sought-after areas (Mafra-Vila, Malveira). In Ericeira, the T2 rent rises to between €900 and €1,300, with a strong tourist component and seasonality.

Housing supply is high in the municipality. For a T3 house with land, rents start at €1,100 in areas such as Sobreiro, Pero Pinheiro or Achada.

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Base scenario: accumulated growth between 12% and 18% inland, taking the median T2 rent to between €850 and €1,000 per month by 2029. In Ericeira, the increase may be higher (15% to 25%) because of tourist and international pressure. Mafra is one of the municipalities in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area that benefits most from remote work and the demand for quality of life.

## Population and density

Mafra had 86,523 residents in the 2021 Census, 12.8% more than in 2011, the largest growth in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area in relative terms. The area is 291.66 km², with a density of 297 inhabitants per km².

The population is growing in every parish, with particular strength in Malveira, Venda do Pinheiro and Mafra-Vila.

## Who lives in Mafra? Resident profile

Mafra attracts three groups. The first is young Portuguese families looking for a house with land at affordable prices and willing to depend on a car. The second is remote workers and creative professionals who moved to Ericeira or the interior of the municipality during and after the pandemic. The third is the international community, mainly in Ericeira (surfers, Nordic and French expatriates).

The Brazilian community presence has been increasing inland.

## Transport and connection to central Lisboa

Mafra has no train. Connections to Lisboa are mainly:

By car along the A8, which is the main option. From Mafra-Vila to central Lisboa it takes around 35 to 50 minutes outside rush hour. During rush hour, the A8 and the CRIL can become congested.

Mafrense coaches connect Mafra, Malveira and Venda do Pinheiro to Lisboa (Campo Grande). The journey takes 50 to 75 minutes.

The lack of a train is the main limitation of the municipality. There are no planned public projects to change this reality.

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

**Póvoa da Galega** — `Ongoing`
Local operation under the Metropolitan Plan to Support Disadvantaged Communities, financed by the Recovery and Resilience Plan.

The City Council has set the implementation of 390 housing solutions and runs a programme that supports the rent of 130 families. There are around 425 families on the waiting list.

There are more than 25 private developments being marketed in the municipality, mainly in Mafra-Vila, Malveira and Ericeira. T2 under construction from €280,000 in the most affordable areas.

The enhancement of historical heritage continues (Mafra National Palace, Tapada de Mafra) with tourism effects.

## Schools, healthcare and retail

In education, there is good coverage of public primary and secondary schools. There are some private colleges in Ericeira and Mafra-Vila. Higher education is in Lisboa.

In healthcare, Loures Hospital (Beatriz Ângelo) is the nearest public reference. In Mafra-Vila and Ericeira there are health centres and private clinics.

In retail, there is a Mafra Retail Park (under construction), smaller shopping centres in Malveira (Continente) and traditional commerce in Mafra-Vila and Ericeira. For larger shopping, the usual trip is to Loures (LoureShopping) or Lisboa.

## FAQ

### Is Mafra good for families?
Very much so. It is one of the most sought-after municipalities in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area for families with children. Mafra-Vila, Malveira and Venda do Pinheiro have good schools, parks and a residential environment. T3 rent between €950 and €1,200 (inland) or €1,300 to €2,000 (Ericeira).

### Is Mafra worth it if I work in Lisboa?
Without a train, it depends heavily on your schedule and your workplace in Lisboa. For flexible schedules, it makes sense. For rigid schedules during rush hour, the time lost in traffic on the A8 and the CRIL can make it a painful option.

### What is it like to live in Ericeira?
Ericeira is one of the most touristy spots in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area. It has strong international presence (especially surfers), lively commerce and restaurants, and a cosmopolitan atmosphere. It is more expensive than the interior of Mafra. In summer, the tourist presence is intense.

### Which are the cheapest areas of Mafra?
Igreja Nova, Cheleiros, Achada and rural areas have T2 rents and houses with land at values significantly below those on the coast.

### What is the Tapada Nacional de Mafra?
It is a hunting reserve and natural park of 819 hectares, with native fauna (fallow deer, wild boar, foxes) and trails for visits. Next to the Mafra National Palace.
