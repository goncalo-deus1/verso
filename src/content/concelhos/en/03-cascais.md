# Living in Cascais: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Cascais is the most expensive municipality in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area, with the average advertised T2 (2-bedroom flat) rent approaching €1,500 and the effective contracted rent between €1,200 and €1,400. It has 214,000 residents, is the preferred destination of the upper-class international community and concentrates the largest offering of international schools in the country. A good choice for those who work remotely or under a hybrid model with few trips to Lisboa. Less suitable for those who need to commute daily to the capital at peak hours without using the train.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Cascais worth living in?

Cascais offers beaches, quality of life, international schools and a growing business ecosystem. In exchange, the cost of living is the highest in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area and the traffic on the A5 and the Marginal at peak hours is hard to avoid. For those willing to pay the premium (and to use the train where possible), Cascais is the best option on the north shore for quality of life.

Carcavelos, Estoril and Cascais-Vila are the most sought-after areas. Alcabideche, São Domingos de Rana and Tires are alternatives with more space for less money.

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Cascais in 2026?

The median rent per square metre in Cascais is €15.31, according to INE (Q4 2024), the second-highest value in the country after Lisboa. For an 80 m² T2, this puts the contracted rent between €1,200 and €1,400 per month.

Values advertised online are significantly higher. Imovirtual's January 2026 Barometer recorded an average advertised rent of €2,500 for Cascais, with a year-on-year rise of 13.6%. The gap between advertised and contracted values is typical of markets with strong international demand, where some owners list properties in Estoril, Quinta da Marinha and Birre well above the median.

The most expensive areas are Quinta da Marinha, Birre, Estoril, Monte Estoril and Cascais-Vila. The most affordable are São Domingos de Rana, Tires, Trajouce and the inland areas of Alcabideche.

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Cascais is one of the markets where Confidencial Imobiliário identifies the greatest risk of a downward correction in the coming years, as it is already out of reach for much of the Portuguese middle class. Base scenario: accumulated growth between 5% and 10%, with the median T2 rent reaching €1,350 to €1,500 in 2029. Alternative scenario: stabilisation or a slight correction in premium areas if international demand pulls back or if the golden visa is definitively revoked.

## Population and density

Cascais had 214,134 residents in the 2021 Census, 3.7% more than in 2011. The area is 97.40 km², which gives a density of around 2,198 inhabitants per km².

The population is growing mainly in the eastern part of the municipality (São Domingos de Rana, Tires, Carcavelos), while Cascais-Vila and Estoril maintain a stable density.

## Who lives in Cascais? Resident profile

Cascais has the most international profile in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area. More than 20% of the resident population is foreign in some parishes, with a strong presence of British, American, Brazilian, French and Nordic residents. Technology and finance companies with offices in the area (from Carcavelos to Quinta da Beloura) attract qualified staff.

Established Portuguese families, self-employed professionals, executives and a foreign retiree community are the other main groups. Housing pressure has been pushing the Portuguese middle class from the coast to the inland part of the municipality.

## Transport and connection to central Lisboa

The Cascais-Cais do Sodré rail line is the main axis. From Cascais station to Cais do Sodré takes around 40 minutes. From Carcavelos to Cais do Sodré, 25 minutes. Frequency is 20 minutes at peak hours.

By car, the A5 (Lisboa-Cascais) is the fast route but has heavy traffic between 8am and 9.30am and between 6pm and 7.30pm. The Marginal (N6 road) is more scenic but equally congested.

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

Cascais has 1,371 families on the waiting list for municipal housing. The City Council runs its own subsidised rent programme and is executing projects from the Recovery and Resilience Plan.

The local authority has identified land for new housing supply, particularly in Alcabideche and São Domingos de Rana, with the aim of retaining the middle class in the municipality.

The regeneration of the riverfront continues, with investments in public and sporting facilities.

Expansions and improvements continue at the Hospital de Cascais, considered one of the best public hospitals in the country under a public-private partnership.

## Schools, healthcare and retail

Cascais has the largest concentration of international schools in Portugal. St. Julian's School (Carcavelos), St. Dominic's International School (São Domingos de Rana), Salesianos do Estoril, Colégio Marista de Carcavelos (Parede) and Externato Nossa Senhora do Rosário are some of the main ones. Public schools have a reputation above the national average, especially those in Carcavelos, Estoril and Cascais-Vila.

In healthcare, the Hospital de Cascais (public-private management via Ribera Salud, concession started in 2023, replacing Lusíadas) is the reference. The Hospital da Luz Oeiras and CUF Cascais complement the private offering.

In retail, CascaiShopping is the largest centre in the western area. The centre of Cascais and Estoril still have an active traditional retail scene. Carcavelos has the Mercado de Carcavelos as a local reference point.

## FAQ

### Is Cascais good for families with children?
It is the best area in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area for families wanting international schools and proximity to the beach. T3 rents start at €1,500 and can easily reach €3,000 or more in premium areas.

### Is Cascais worth living in if I do not work nearby?
It makes more sense for those who work remotely or under a hybrid model. For people who need to commute daily to Lisboa-Saldanha or Lisboa-Marquês, the total round-trip commute is 1h15 to 1h30 at peak hours.

### Which are the cheapest areas in Cascais?
Tires, Trajouce, parts of Alcabideche and the inland areas of São Domingos de Rana keep T2 rents between €900 and €1,100. They are further from the beach and the train station.

### Cascais or Oeiras: which is better?
Cascais has more beaches, more international schools and a more residential profile. Oeiras has more offices, a better connection to central Lisboa and slightly lower rents. For international families, Cascais. For professionals who work in Lisboa five days a week, Oeiras.

### Is there a risk that Cascais rents will fall?
Cascais is one of the markets where Confidencial Imobiliário identifies the highest probability of stabilisation or a downward correction, as it is out of reach for a significant share of domestic demand. Changes to the golden visa or the non-habitual resident IRS regime may have an impact.
