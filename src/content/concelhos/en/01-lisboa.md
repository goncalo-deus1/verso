# Living in Lisboa: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Lisboa is the central municipality of the Lisbon Metropolitan Area, with around 545,000 residents and the highest rents in the country. A T2 (2-bedroom flat) costs on average between €1,450 and €1,600 per month. It is the right choice for people who prioritise proximity to everything, an intense cultural life and dense public transport. It is the wrong choice for those looking for space, a private garden, or a rental budget below €1,300 per month.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Lisboa worth living in?

It depends on your benchmark. If the comparison is Cascais or Oeiras, Lisboa is more expensive per square metre of rent but offers the densest transport network, cultural amenities and employment opportunities in the country. If the comparison is Almada, Seixal or Loures, you pay a meaningful premium in exchange for not crossing the Tejo nor depending on the train.

For people working in Lisboa three or more days a week, living inside the municipality pays back in time and quality of life. For families with children who prioritise a larger home and a garden, neighbouring municipalities are usually the better choice.

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Lisboa in 2026?

The median rent for a T2 in Lisboa settled at €1,586 in Q3 2025, according to Confidencial Imobiliário's Residential Information System. The figure covers flats of around 80 m² across a range of areas of the municipality.

Variation by parish is significant. In the historic centre and Avenidas Novas, a T2 rarely drops below €1,800. In Marvila, Beato, Lumiar or Carnide, it is still possible to find T2s between €1,200 and €1,400. The highest prices are in Santo António, Estrela, Misericórdia and Parque das Nações.

In €/m², INE recorded €15.93 for new contracts in Q4 2024 — the highest value in the country. The parishes of Santa Maria Maior, Campo de Ourique and Estrela already exceed €17 per square metre.

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Lisboa's rental market started to stabilise in 2025. Confidencial Imobiliário registered in Q3 2025 the second consecutive positive quarterly variation (+0.6%), after a year-on-year drop of 2.9%. The Idealista index confirmed this moderation, with Lisboa closing 2025 at +1.2% annual growth.

Base scenario for 2026–2029: accumulated growth between 8% and 12%, putting the median T2 rent between €1,700 and €1,770 per month by 2029. The scenario assumes a stabilising median wage, no regulatory shocks and a gradual influx of new supply under the Construir Portugal programme and the Parque Cidades do Tejo. Pessimistic scenario: accumulated growth above 18% if supply does not arrive in time to absorb domestic and international demand.

## Population and density

The municipality of Lisboa had 544,851 residents in the 2021 Census, 1.4% less than in 2011. The area is 100.05 km², which gives a density of about 5,446 inhabitants per km² — the highest in mainland Portugal.

Despite the overall loss, parishes such as Arroios, Areeiro, Avenidas Novas, Alvalade, Santa Clara and Parque das Nações have grown. The historic-centre parishes (Misericórdia, Santa Maria Maior, São Vicente, Santo António) lost more than 8,000 residents in the last decade, with Misericórdia recording a 26% drop.

## Who lives in Lisboa? Resident profile

Lisboa attracts three main groups. The first is young professionals between 25 and 40 working in the capital's offices, especially in Avenidas Novas, Saldanha and Parque das Nações. They look for T1 or T2 flats and prioritise access to the metro. The second is the international community, with a strong presence of Americans, Brazilians, French and Indians, concentrated in Santo António, Príncipe Real, Estrela and Parque das Nações. The third is long-time residents, often on regulated old-rent contracts, spread across the peripheral parishes.

Families with young children have been moving out. More than 55,000 people left Lisboa between 2011 and 2021, 78% of whom moved to peripheral municipalities, according to INE data.

## Transport and connection to the city centre

When you are inside the municipality, "centre" means Baixa, Avenida da Liberdade or Marquês de Pombal. From any parish, the average commute to the centre is 15 to 30 minutes by metro or bus.

The metro network has four lines (Blue, Yellow, Green, Red) and is being expanded. Projects to watch:

**Circular Metro Line** — `Q1 2027`
Connects Rato to Cais do Sodré, creating the new Estrela and Santos stations. Opening expected in the first quarter of 2027, according to the President of Metropolitano de Lisboa.

**Red Line (extension)** — `Post-2030`
Extends from São Sebastião to Alcântara, with four new stations: Amoreiras, Campo de Ourique, Infante Santo and Alcântara. The contract was awarded to Mota-Engil and Spie Batignolles for €321.9 million; full operation is not expected before 2030.

**LIOS** — `Under study`
The Sustainable Intermodal Line will connect Lisboa to Oeiras to the west and Santa Apolónia to Sacavém to the east. Under study, no confirmed date.

For regional travel, Oriente, Santa Apolónia, Cais do Sodré, Rossio and Sete Rios stations concentrate suburban rail services to Sintra, Cascais, Setúbal and Azambuja, plus long-distance services and the planned high-speed rail to Madrid.

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

Four projects with direct impact on the property market and quality of life:

**Humberto Delgado Airport land** — `Post-2034`
At 477 hectares, this is the city's largest land reserve. Parque Cidades do Tejo, S.A. will develop these plots once the airport closes (likely between 2034 and 2037), with proposals being studied for affordable housing, green space and a new urban centrality.

**Beato digital hub** — `Ongoing`
The Beato Innovation District has been attracting international tech companies and is driving housing demand in the eastern part of the city.

Lisboa has about 15,700 families on the waiting list for municipal housing. The City Council is executing projects from the Recovery and Resilience Plan to rehabilitate and build new supply over the next two to three years.

The ongoing revision of the Master Plan (PDM) foresees higher residential density in urban transformation zones such as Marvila, Beato, Braço de Prata and Vale de Chelas.

## Schools, healthcare and retail

Lisboa concentrates a large share of the country's public and private amenities. In education, there are around 50 public basic and secondary schools, three structural university clusters (Universidade de Lisboa, Universidade Nova, ISCTE) and international private schools — Lycée Français Charles Lepierre (Campolide), Colégio Luso-Suíço (Estrela), Escola Alemã de Lisboa (Telheiras) and United Lisbon International School (Parque das Nações). In healthcare, the municipality hosts the university hospitals of Santa Maria, São José, Curry Cabral and Estefânia, plus three large private networks (Lusíadas, CUF, Luz). In retail, there is everything: hypermarkets in almost every parish, municipal markets (Campo de Ourique, Arroios, Ribeira), large shopping centres (Colombo, Vasco da Gama, Amoreiras) and traditional commercial streets still alive in Alvalade, Lapa and Bairro Alto.

## FAQ

### Is Lisboa good for families with children?
It depends on the area. Alvalade, Avenidas Novas, Telheiras and Parque das Nações have good school offerings and parks. The historic centre is less practical for families due to a lack of nearby green spaces and difficulty parking. The minimum budget for a T3 rental in a family-friendly area starts at €1,800.

### Is Lisboa good for remote workers?
Yes, especially in areas with concentrated coffee shops and co-working spaces (Príncipe Real, Santos, Marvila). Fibre coverage is good in almost every parish.

### Which are the cheapest areas to rent in Lisboa?
Marvila, Beato, Olivais, Carnide, Charneca and Santa Clara keep T2 rents between €1,150 and €1,400. They are areas with public transport but further from the historic centre.

### Is it worth buying in Lisboa in 2026?
The median sale price was €4,492 per square metre in Q1 2025, according to INE. For an 80 m² T2, that means about €360,000. It makes sense for horizons of 7 to 10 years. For shorter horizons, renting is more flexible.

### Will the airport be moved out of Lisboa?
Yes, Humberto Delgado Airport is expected to close between 2034 and 2037, when the new Luís de Camões Airport in Alcochete becomes operational. The 477 hectares released will be subject to an urban plan currently under study.
