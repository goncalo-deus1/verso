# Living in Sintra: what you need to know before choosing this area

**Quick summary:** Sintra is the second-largest municipality of the Lisbon Metropolitan Area by population, with around 386,000 residents. The median rent for a T2 (2-bedroom flat) is between €850 and €950, cheaper than Lisboa, Cascais or Oeiras. It is a good choice for families looking for a larger home for less money and willing to commute by train. It is less suitable for those who need to reach central Lisboa in under 40 minutes at peak hours.

*Updated on 30 April 2026.*

---

## Is Sintra worth living in?

Sintra covers a huge area and contains very different realities. The Lisboa-Sintra rail line concentrates most of the population in Cacém, Massamá, Queluz, Mira-Sintra, Algueirão and Mem Martins. The historic town and the sierra area have a more rural and tourist-driven character. The coastal strip (Colares, Almoçageme, Praia das Maçãs) is more expensive and more sought after by remote workers.

For people working in Lisboa who take the train, it makes sense. For those who depend on a car to reach the capital, traffic on the A37 and the IC19 at peak hours is a heavy burden.

## How much does it cost to rent a T2 in Sintra in 2026?

The median rent per square metre in Sintra settled at €10.82 in Q4 2024, according to INE, with a year-on-year rise of 9.7%. For an average 80 m² T2, this puts monthly rent between €850 and €950 in the most sought-after areas (Cacém, Queluz, Massamá), and between €750 and €850 in areas further from the train station.

The highest values in the municipality are in the Quinta da Beloura area, in Sintra-São Pedro and in detached houses near the sierra. The lowest values are in Algueirão and Mem Martins outside the main urban centres.

### Three-year T2 rent projection (2026 to 2029)

Sintra has been one of the areas with the strongest upward pressure in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area, benefiting from the spillover effect of people priced out of Lisboa, Oeiras and Amadora. The year-on-year rise of 9.7% in 2024 and the sustained pressure in 2025 point to a base scenario of accumulated growth between 12% and 18% by 2029. The median T2 rent should sit between €1,000 and €1,100 per month over that horizon. The main risk is that the arrival of new private developments (especially in Massamá, Queluz and Cacém) may adjust this scenario slightly downward, particularly in 2027 and 2028.

## Population and density

Sintra had 385,954 residents in the 2021 Census, 2.1% more than in 2011. The area is 319.23 km², which gives a density of about 1,209 inhabitants per km². The actual density is much higher in the parishes along the rail line and much lower in the sierra and the northern coast.

## Who lives in Sintra? Resident profile

Sintra mainly attracts young families and middle-class residents looking for more square metres for less money than in Lisboa. The presence of Brazilian, Ukrainian and Cape Verdean communities is significant in the parishes along the rail line (Cacém, Massamá, Queluz). The sierra area (Sintra-Vila, Colares, Almoçageme) attracts a different profile: established families, upper-class expatriates and creative professionals who prioritise quality of life and are willing to depend on a car.

Demographic pressure is concentrated in the parishes with a train station. Cacém, Algueirão-Mem Martins, Massamá and Queluz are among the most densely populated in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area.

## Transport and connection to central Lisboa

The Lisboa-Sintra rail line is the structural axis. From Sintra station to Rossio is around 40 minutes. From Cacém station to Rossio is 25 to 30 minutes. From Portela de Sintra it is 35 minutes. Frequency is 10 to 20 minutes at peak hours, but the line frequently suffers from overcrowding.

**Mira-Sintra station** — `Ongoing`
Refurbishment integrated into the modernisation works on the Sintra line, with improvements to comfort and train capacity.

By car, the IC19 is the fast route but has heavy traffic between 8am and 9.30am and between 6pm and 7.30pm. The A37 (Radial de Sintra) links to the IC19 and the A5 and is in better condition.

The future LIOS Ocidental (Sustainable Intermodal Line) is under study and should link Oeiras to Alcântara, creating a transport axis that may benefit alternative connections from Sintra in the long term.

## Planned urban projects in the municipality

Sintra has around 4,000 families on the waiting list for municipal housing, the second-largest number in the Lisbon Metropolitan Area after Lisboa. The City Council is executing projects from the Recovery and Resilience Plan to rehabilitate and build new supply.

There are several dozen new private developments under construction or on sale, particularly in Massamá, Queluz, Belas and Algueirão. New-build T2 prices start at €280,000 and can reach €400,000 in premium areas.

The ongoing revision of the Master Plan (PDM) foresees new urban expansion zones near the train stations, anticipating the housing pressure of the coming years.

## Schools, healthcare and retail

In education, Sintra has good coverage of public basic and secondary schools, and two important private education hubs (TASIS Portugal in Sintra-São Pedro, Carlucci International in Sintra). University presence is limited and most university students travel to Lisboa.

In healthcare, the Hospital Professor Doutor Fernando Fonseca (Amadora-Sintra) is the public reference and also serves Amadora. The private offering includes the Hospital CUF Sintra and smaller clinics.

In retail, the municipality has CascaiShopping (on the border with Cascais), Forum Sintra and dozens of hypermarkets. The traditional shopping streets are concentrated in Sintra-Vila, Mem Martins and Queluz.

## FAQ

### Is Sintra good for families with children?
Yes. The areas of Massamá, Belas, Sintra-São Pedro and Mira-Sintra have good public and private school options and are more residential and quiet. T3 rents range between €1,000 and €1,300 per month.

### Is Sintra worth living in if I work in Lisboa?
It makes sense if you can use the train. The line runs every 10 to 20 minutes. If you depend on a car every day, time lost in traffic significantly reduces the benefit of paying less rent.

### Which are the cheapest areas in Sintra?
Algueirão-Mem Martins, outside the central nuclei, and Almargem do Bispo have T2 rents below €800. They are further from the main station.

### What is the difference between living in Sintra-Vila and along the rail line?
Sintra-Vila and the sierra are more expensive, more tourist-oriented and more car-dependent. The rail-line parishes (Cacém, Queluz, Massamá, Algueirão) are more urban, denser and better served by public transport.

### Which projects may change Sintra in the coming years?
Most of the planned transformation is residential, with new private developments coming onto the market. The LIOS, under study, may create new transport options in the long term. The Recovery and Resilience Plan is financing public housing rehabilitation.
